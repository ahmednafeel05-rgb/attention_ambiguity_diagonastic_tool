A.03.2 · Pronoun resolution in GPT-2 small and GPT-2 XL

Who is “it”?

“The knife cut the bread easily because it was so sharp.” Change one word to soft and “it” means the bread. GPT-2 XL has one attention head, layer 20 head 4, that moves with that word: 74% of its attention lands on knife, then 97% on bread. GPT-2 small has no head that does this reliably.

What the attention showed

One head swings with the clue, and only in the bigger model

Each pair is two sentences that differ by one word, and the word decides what the pronoun means. We asked each model which reading it prefers, then read the attention from that final clue word back to the two nouns. The first five pairs were used to find the heads. The other nine were held back and tested afterwards.

Both models fail

Trophy and suitcase

…because it was too big / small

Both models answer “suitcase” for both versions, the noun nearest to “it”. Head L20H4 agrees: 50% on suitcase after “big”, 61% after “small”. At the word “it” itself, attention is identical in both sentences, because GPT-2 reads left to right and has not seen the clue yet.

Resolved · held-out

Knife and bread

…because it was so sharp / soft

GPT-2 XL answers both versions correctly. L20H4 puts 74% of its attention on “knife” after “sharp” and 97% on “bread” after “soft”, a swing of +1.65 on a sentence it was not selected on. GPT-2 small also gets this pair right.

Moves, never switches · held-out

Lion and deer

…because it was so fast / slow

GPT-2 XL answers “deer” both times, so it is wrong for “fast”. L20H4 moves the right way, from 88% on deer down to 52%, with lion rising from 4% to 24%. It never crosses over, and neither does the answer.

Swing = (pull toward the first noun with the first clue) − (the same with the second clue). It runs from −2 to +2. Positive means the head moves toward the correct noun each time.

How this was measured

Method and limits

  • Models. GPT-2 small (12 layers × 12 heads) and GPT-2 XL (48 layers × 25 heads), loaded with Hugging Face Transformers. Attention weights are the model’s real values, exported once and shown here as-is.
  • What each model answers. “It” is replaced by each noun in turn (“the knife was so sharp”, “the bread was so sharp”). The sentence the model finds more likely is its answer.
  • Where attention is read. From the final clue word, the first point where the model has seen it. Reading from “it” cannot work: the two versions of the sentence are identical up to that point.
  • Finding the head. Heads were ranked on two pairs (man/son, fish/worm) and then checked on nine new pairs. L20H4 held up. Another candidate, L14H24, did not, which is why the held-out check matters.
  • Limits. Fourteen pairs is a small set, and the sentences are fixed rather than typed in, because running GPT-2 XL needs a GPU. Attention shows where a head looks, not why the model decides. Showing that L20H4 causes the answer would take an ablation or patching experiment.
Data: real attention weights from GPT-2 small and GPT-2 XL on 14 Winograd-style sentence pairs. Built for artifact A.03.2.
